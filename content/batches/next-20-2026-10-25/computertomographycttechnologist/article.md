# Computer Tomography (CT) Technologist

## Career Overview

A computed tomography technologist produces detailed images of the inside of the body using a CT scanner. The scanner uses x-rays and computer processing to build cross-sectional views, rather like viewing the body one thin layer at a time. Clinicians use these images to investigate injuries, disease, and other medical concerns.

The technologist prepares the patient and performs the examination. A radiologist, a physician trained in interpreting medical images, evaluates the findings. The job joins technical decisions with direct patient care: a clear image depends partly on helping a real person remain safely positioned and understand the instructions. [FDA CT explanation](https://www.fda.gov/radiation-emitting-products/medical-x-ray-imaging/computed-tomography-ct); [RadiologyInfo](https://www.radiologyinfo.org/en/info/bodyct)

## Duties and Responsibilities

CT work begins before the scanner moves. Technologists check the examination request, confirm the patient's identity, gather relevant information, and prepare the room. They position the patient, select the appropriate examination protocol within their responsibilities, and explain what the patient will experience.

Some examinations use contrast material to make particular structures easier to distinguish. Preparation and administration depend on orders, screening, training, and local authority. The technologist monitors the patient, reviews the acquired images for technical quality, and completes the examination record. The goal is useful diagnostic information with an appropriate radiation exposure, not simply collecting the largest number of images. [ASRT practice standards](https://www.asrt.org/docs/default-source/practice-standards/ps_computedtomography.pdf?sfvrsn=9e076d0_24)

## A Day in the Life

In an illustrative hospital shift, a technologist first reviews scheduled examinations and checks that the scanner is ready. One patient is worried about an abdominal scan, so the technologist explains positioning and rehearses a brief breathing instruction. After acquisition, the technologist checks whether the images cover the intended anatomy and sends the examination for interpretation.

An emergency patient then needs imaging, changing the planned order. The technologist coordinates with the clinical team and prepares for a patient whose mobility differs from the earlier patient's. This example illustrates changing priorities, not a timetable shared by all CT departments. [ARRT description of CT work](https://www.arrt.org/pages/resources/earn-additional-credentials/earn-additional-credentials-postprimary/computed-tomography); [ASRT standards](https://www.asrt.org/docs/default-source/practice-standards/ps_computedtomography.pdf?sfvrsn=9e076d0_24)

## Work Environment and Schedule

CT technologists work in hospitals and imaging centers, among other imaging services. A hospital may need scans around the clock, while an outpatient center may concentrate on scheduled appointments. Radiologists, nurses, transport staff, and referring clinicians all contribute to the patient's visit.

Much of the job alternates between the examination room and the scanner's control area. Working at a console does not eliminate patient contact: positioning, transfers, explanations, and observation remain central. [ARRT](https://www.arrt.org/pages/resources/earn-additional-credentials/earn-additional-credentials-postprimary/computed-tomography); [BLS](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/radiologic-technologists.htm)

## Skills, Knowledge, and Personal Qualities

Anatomy knowledge helps a technologist understand which structures must be visible. Physics helps explain how acquisition choices influence image quality and radiation dose. Computer skills support image reconstruction and handling, but the equipment does not remove the need for professional judgment.

A good explanation is another technical tool. Instructions must be short enough for a patient to follow while uncomfortable or anxious. Students who enjoy both spatial problems and explaining a process to someone else may find this combination appealing. [ASRT careers](https://www.asrt.org/main/career-center/careers-in-radiologic-technology); [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/radiation-emitting-products/medical-x-ray-imaging/computed-tomography-ct)

## Physical and Emotional Demands

The work includes standing, reaching, and helping patients move safely. Some patients are injured, in pain, or attached to equipment that must be managed with the care team. Patient-transfer techniques and asking for assistance protect both the patient and staff.

CT uses ionizing radiation. Technologists follow radiation-protection practices and work to avoid unnecessary exposure while obtaining the information the examination needs. Contrast-related preparation adds another reason to follow the department's screening and response procedures carefully. [BLS](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/radiologic-technologists.htm); [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/radiation-emitting-products/medical-x-ray-imaging/computed-tomography-ct)

## Education and Training

CT is often an additional specialization after preparation in another imaging discipline. Under ARRT's postprimary pathway, a candidate first holds certification and registration in radiography, nuclear medicine technology, or radiation therapy. Nuclear medicine credentials may be through ARRT or NMTCB.

The CT pathway then requires structured education, clinical experience, ethics compliance, and an examination. A short course alone is therefore not the entire pathway. High-school biology, physics, and mathematics can support the foundation, while the professional program supplies supervised patient-care experience. [ARRT CT pathway](https://www.arrt.org/pages/resources/earn-additional-credentials/earn-additional-credentials-postprimary/computed-tomography)

## Certification and Licensure

ARRT certification and registration recognize professional qualifications; state licensure is a separate legal matter. Kansas K.S.A. 65-7303 requires a license to practice radiologic technology except as otherwise provided in the act. Section 65-7304 includes exemptions for specified circumstances, including certain supervised and trained personnel.

A prospective CT technologist should therefore match the actual position and duties to the Kansas requirements and the employer's credential expectations. Neither holding a CT credential nor noticing an exemption is enough to decide every person's legal authority without considering the statutory conditions. [Kansas requirement](https://www.kslegislature.gov/b2025_26/laws/065_000_0000_chapter/065_073_0000_article/065_073_0003_section/065_073_0003_k/); [Kansas exceptions](https://www.kslegislature.gov/b2025_26/laws/065_000_0000_chapter/065_073_0000_article/065_073_0004_section/065_073_0004_k/); [ARRT](https://www.arrt.org/pages/earn-arrt-credentials/credential-options/computed-tomography)

## Salary, Entry-Level Earnings, and Job Outlook

BLS reports a **May 2025 U.S. median annual wage of $80,110 for radiologic technologists and technicians**. CT technologists are part of this broader group; this is not a CT-only salary estimate or a starting wage. BLS projects **5% growth for radiologic technologists and technicians from 2025 to 2035**.

When comparing an actual offer, look at whether the position requires CT credentials, experience in another modality, weekend coverage, or overnight work. The national median cannot answer those job-specific questions, and it should not be read as a promised Kansas wage. [BLS pay and projections](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/radiologic-technologists.htm)

## Specialties and Career Advancement

CT experience can develop around particular examinations, patient groups, or responsibilities. Some technologists become especially experienced with emergency imaging or specialized cardiovascular examinations. Technical expertise may also support work in education, quality improvement, or department leadership.

Learning another imaging modality requires that modality's preparation. CT experience alone does not automatically qualify someone to perform MRI, ultrasound, or radiation therapy. Comparing credential pathways is useful when planning a career that might eventually include more than one imaging service. [ASRT careers](https://www.asrt.org/main/career-center/careers-in-radiologic-technology); [RadiologyInfo CT uses](https://www.radiologyinfo.org/en/info/bodyct)

## Benefits and Challenges of the Career

The career offers a visible result of careful work: an image that helps a physician answer a clinical question. It can appeal to people who want direct patient interaction but also enjoy instruments, anatomy, and computer-based problem solving.

The challenge is balancing those interests under practical constraints. A patient may not be able to move as requested; an urgent case may interrupt the schedule; an image may need technical review before the examination is complete. Satisfaction comes from handling those details well, rather than from operating a scanner alone. [ASRT standards](https://www.asrt.org/docs/default-source/practice-standards/ps_computedtomography.pdf?sfvrsn=9e076d0_24)

## Career Connections and Related Occupations

Radiography and CT both use x-rays, but CT builds cross-sectional image sets. MRI uses magnetic fields and radio waves instead of x-rays. Ultrasound uses sound waves. These differences affect equipment, safety practices, and professional preparation.

The radiologist's role also differs from the technologist's: the physician interprets the images in the medical context. A student who likes creating high-quality examinations may prefer the technologist's work; someone interested in physician diagnosis would be considering a different educational path. [FDA CT overview](https://www.fda.gov/radiation-emitting-products/medical-x-ray-imaging/computed-tomography-ct); [ASRT careers](https://www.asrt.org/main/career-center/careers-in-radiologic-technology); [RadiologyInfo](https://www.radiologyinfo.org/en/info/bodyct)

## Could This Career Interest You?

Do you enjoy imagining how a three-dimensional object would look in cross-section? Would you find satisfaction in improving a patient's understanding of a short but unfamiliar procedure? Can you stay interested in careful preparation even when the image acquisition itself is fast?

A conversation with a CT technologist can explore how much of a shift is spent positioning, checking information, reviewing images, and communicating. Those details often reveal more about career fit than watching the scanner operate. [ASRT standards](https://www.asrt.org/docs/default-source/practice-standards/ps_computedtomography.pdf?sfvrsn=9e076d0_24)

## Professional Associations

The American Society of Radiologic Technologists provides professional education and career information for imaging and radiation-therapy professionals, including CT technologists. It offers a useful way to learn how imaging disciplines relate to one another.

ARRT is the credentialing organization that administers the CT certification and registration pathway. Association participation supports professional learning, while credential eligibility and state legal requirements serve different purposes. [ASRT](https://www.asrt.org/main/career-center/careers-in-radiologic-technology); [ARRT](https://www.arrt.org/pages/earn-arrt-credentials/credential-options/computed-tomography)

# Medical Laboratory Technician

## Career Overview

A medical laboratory technician, often called an MLT, tests patient specimens to help the healthcare team investigate disease and monitor care. The specimen might be blood, urine, or another body material. The technician uses instruments and laboratory methods to turn that specimen into reliable information.

An MLT does more than load tubes into a machine. The work includes checking test quality, recognizing when something needs review, and keeping results connected to the correct patient. Medical laboratory scientists and technicians work together, but their education and assigned responsibilities can differ. [BLS laboratory roles](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/clinical-laboratory-technologists-and-technicians.htm); [ASCLS scope of practice](https://ascls.org/scope-of-practice/)

## Duties and Responsibilities

Depending on the laboratory, technicians may perform blood counts, chemistry tests, urine testing, microbiology procedures, or testing related to transfusion. They prepare and operate equipment, follow established methods, enter results, and check that quality-control findings are acceptable.

A specimen and a result both require scrutiny. The number produced by an analyzer is useful only when the sample and testing process are suitable. Technicians work within their documented training and responsibilities, involving a supervisor or other qualified colleague when a result or instrument problem needs further investigation. [O*NET laboratory technician tasks](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/29-2012.00); [ASCLS professional responsibilities](https://ascls.org/scope-of-practice/)

## A Day in the Life

In an illustrative hospital-laboratory shift, an MLT receives a handoff about pending specimens and equipment status. Before reporting patient results from an assigned analyzer, the technician checks the required quality-control records. A specimen with incomplete identification needs clarification rather than a guess about who supplied it.

Later, an unexpected control result interrupts the planned testing. The technician follows the laboratory's corrective-action process and communicates the delay. Before leaving, they document unfinished work so the next shift can continue safely. This example represents the kind of reasoning used in laboratory work, not a report of a particular patient's care. [CMS competency guidance](https://www.cms.gov/regulations-and-guidance/legislation/clia/downloads/clia_compbrochure_508.pdf); [O*NET](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/29-2012.00)

## Work Environment and Schedule

MLTs work in hospitals, diagnostic laboratories, medical offices, and other testing services. A small laboratory may need a technician to work across several areas, while a larger service may organize staff by testing department.

Patient contact depends on the job. Some technicians collect specimens; others mainly work with material delivered to the laboratory. Hospitals may need coverage at night, on weekends, and on holidays. Someone interested in laboratory science should examine both the testing assignments and the schedule of a prospective position. [BLS work environment](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/clinical-laboratory-technologists-and-technicians.htm)

## Skills, Knowledge, and Personal Qualities

Biology and chemistry help a technician understand what is being measured and why a test has limitations. Mathematics supports calculations and careful preparation of solutions. Computer skills are needed for instruments and laboratory information systems, while hand skills remain important for handling samples and equipment.

An especially valuable habit is checking the process before accepting a result. Clear documentation lets another worker understand what was done, and clear communication helps the clinical team know when a result requires attention. [ASCLS education](https://ascls.org/how-do-i-become-a-laboratory-professional/); [O*NET](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/29-2012.00)

## Physical and Emotional Demands

Laboratory work involves biological specimens, chemicals, and equipment that require protective procedures. Depending on the station, a technician may stand for long periods or repeat fine hand movements. Following specimen-handling rules and using appropriate protective equipment are part of normal practice.

The emotional pressure may be less visible than in bedside care, but results can influence important decisions. A technician must be comfortable acknowledging a mistake or stopping a questionable process. Quiet work still carries responsibility to the person behind the specimen. [BLS demands](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/clinical-laboratory-technologists-and-technicians.htm); [O*NET work context](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/29-2012.00)

## Education and Training

An associate-degree MLT program is a common educational route. Coursework combines general education, laboratory science, and supervised practical experience. Students learn the methods and quality practices used in areas such as chemistry, hematology, microbiology, and blood banking.

ASCP's current MLT Route 1 requires an associate degree or 60 semester hours of college credit, plus completion of a NAACLS- or ABHES-accredited MLT program within the previous five years. Those are combined conditions, not alternatives that let any associate degree replace laboratory preparation. Other eligibility routes have their own requirements. [ASCLS education](https://ascls.org/how-do-i-become-a-laboratory-professional/); [ASCP MLT](https://www.ascp.org/boc/explore-credentials/view-all-credentials/MLT)

## Certification and Licensure

The ASCP Board of Certification offers the MLT credential after applicants meet an eligible pathway and pass its examination. Employers may specify professional certification and additional qualifications for a position. Certification documents an individual's achievement; it is different from the laboratory's authorization to perform testing.

CLIA establishes federal laboratory quality standards. CMS explains that moderate- and high-complexity laboratories must demonstrate and document testing personnel's competency. Kansas's CLIA program is administered by KDHE's Laboratory Improvement Program Office. A laboratory's CLIA certificate does not replace the employee's education, required competency, or any applicable individual requirements. [ASCP](https://www.ascp.org/boc/explore-credentials/view-all-credentials/MLT); [CMS](https://www.cms.gov/regulations-and-guidance/legislation/clia/downloads/clia_compbrochure_508.pdf); [KDHE](https://www.kdhe.ks.gov/1114/Clinical-Laboratory-Improvement-Amendmen)

## Salary, Entry-Level Earnings, and Job Outlook

The BLS **May 2025 U.S. median annual wage of $62,930** covers **clinical laboratory technologists and technicians together**. It includes both technician and scientist-level roles, so it is not an MLT-only wage, a Kansas figure, or a verified starting salary.

BLS projects **3% growth from 2025 to 2035** for that combined group. For an entry opportunity, compare postings that specifically describe MLT qualifications and duties. A position requiring a bachelor's-level scientist credential should not be treated as an interchangeable salary example for an associate-level technician position. [BLS wage and outlook](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/clinical-laboratory-technologists-and-technicians.htm)

## Specialties and Career Advancement

A technician may develop deeper experience in a particular testing area while remaining an MLT. Additional education can also support a move toward medical laboratory scientist work. That progression requires meeting the new role's education and credential requirements rather than simply accumulating time on the job.

Quality coordination, staff training, or supervisory responsibilities may become possibilities as qualifications and experience grow. Students comparing programs can ask how previous credits transfer and which further credentials a bridge pathway actually supports. [ASCLS education pathways](https://ascls.org/how-do-i-become-a-laboratory-professional/); [ASCLS personnel standards](https://ascls.org/scope-of-practice/)

## Benefits and Challenges of the Career

MLT work can be satisfying for someone who likes a concrete scientific question and a process for answering it. The contribution reaches many patients, including people the technician never meets. A reliable result depends on someone taking the routine details seriously.

The challenge is that speed and accuracy must coexist. Automated instruments can process work quickly, but a questionable control or specimen still needs attention. Someone who enjoys troubleshooting and consistent procedures may appreciate the work more than someone seeking constant public interaction. [O*NET responsibilities](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/29-2012.00); [ASCLS scope](https://ascls.org/scope-of-practice/)

## Career Connections and Related Occupations

“Clinical laboratory technician” is also used for this general kind of work, but employers' titles do not always describe identical qualifications. Compare the duties, education, and certification requested by the position.

A phlebotomist concentrates on collecting blood specimens. A histotechnologist specializes in preparing tissue for examination. A medical laboratory scientist generally has bachelor's-level preparation and may take on more complex analytical or supervisory responsibilities. These colleagues contribute at different points in the diagnostic process; none makes careful specimen handling optional. [BLS role comparisons](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/clinical-laboratory-technologists-and-technicians.htm); [ASCLS personnel standards](https://ascls.org/scope-of-practice/)

## Could This Career Interest You?

Do you enjoy following a procedure closely enough to notice when something does not fit? Would you rather investigate an unexpected result than hide it to finish sooner? Can you find meaning in work that helps a patient without requiring a long bedside relationship?

When talking with an MLT program, ask how students practice quality control and communicate testing problems during clinical experience. These questions connect an interest in science with the actual habits that make laboratory results trustworthy. [CMS competency guidance](https://www.cms.gov/regulations-and-guidance/legislation/clia/downloads/clia_compbrochure_508.pdf); [ASCLS education](https://ascls.org/how-do-i-become-a-laboratory-professional/)

## Professional Associations

The American Society for Clinical Laboratory Science offers education, professional communities, and resources about laboratory careers. Its materials help explain differences among laboratory roles and the importance of quality throughout testing.

The American Society for Clinical Pathology is another professional organization in laboratory medicine, and its Board of Certification administers the MLT credential. Membership, program accreditation, individual certification, and laboratory regulation each serve different purposes. Learning those distinctions helps students evaluate a program's claims. [ASCLS](https://ascls.org/); [ASCP MLT](https://www.ascp.org/boc/explore-credentials/view-all-credentials/MLT)

# Histotechnologist

## Career Overview

A histotechnologist prepares tissue so a pathologist can examine it for disease. A small piece of tissue from a biopsy or surgery cannot simply be placed under a microscope and expected to show every important detail. It must be preserved, supported, cut into thin sections, and treated with appropriate stains.

This is the science of making tissue structures visible while keeping them linked to the correct patient and specimen. Histotechnologists also investigate technical problems and may help evaluate new laboratory methods. Their work supplies reliable material for diagnosis; the pathologist makes the medical interpretation. [National Society for Histotechnology](https://www.nsh.org/about/about-histotechnology); [O*NET](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/29-2011.04)

## Duties and Responsibilities

A typical tissue workflow includes fixation to preserve the sample, processing to prepare it for embedding, and embedding in a supporting block. A microtome cuts thin sections that are placed on slides. Staining then makes particular structures easier to distinguish.

The technologist also checks slide quality, prepares reagents, maintains instruments, and keeps records. If a section is damaged or a stain does not perform properly, the problem must be investigated before it undermines interpretation. Good preparation is more than producing a colorful slide: the tissue must remain suitable for the clinical question. [NSH tissue preparation](https://www.nsh.org/about/about-histotechnology); [O*NET duties](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/29-2011.04)

## A Day in the Life

In an illustrative shift, a histotechnologist reviews the day's tissue work and checks labels against the laboratory record. At the cutting station, they prepare sections from a block, then inspect whether the tissue is intact on the slide. A staining run includes control material to show that the procedure worked as intended.

Later, a control does not look right. The technologist follows the laboratory's troubleshooting process rather than releasing the slides as if the run were acceptable. The shift closes with records and preparation for the next stage of work. This example highlights quality decisions, not an observed patient's case. [O*NET tasks](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/29-2011.04); [ASCP HTL practice areas](https://www.ascp.org/boc/explore-credentials/view-all-credentials/HTL)

## Work Environment and Schedule

Histology work occurs in hospital, private diagnostic, and research laboratories, among other settings. The main work area is the laboratory bench rather than a patient bedside. However, the materials still represent people whose diagnoses may depend on the result.

Schedules depend on the service and its specimen flow. A hospital laboratory supporting surgery may organize work differently from a research laboratory processing planned studies. Students should ask about early starts, weekend coverage, and urgent work at a particular employer rather than assuming every laboratory keeps office hours. [Mayo Clinic histology overview](https://college.mayo.edu/academics/explore-health-care-careers/careers-a-z/histology-technician/); [BLS laboratory settings](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/clinical-laboratory-technologists-and-technicians.htm)

## Skills, Knowledge, and Personal Qualities

Histotechnology rewards careful hands and an understanding of chemistry and anatomy. How tissue is preserved and stained changes what can be seen. A technologist needs to connect an unexpected appearance with possible causes in preparation, reagents, or equipment.

Patience is useful when a delicate section folds or an instrument needs adjustment. Recordkeeping is equally important: an excellent slide with the wrong identification cannot serve its purpose. The career joins visual attention, mechanical skill, and scientific reasoning. [NSH skills](https://www.nsh.org/about/about-histotechnology); [ASCP HTL practice areas](https://www.ascp.org/boc/explore-credentials/view-all-credentials/HTL)

## Physical and Emotional Demands

Repeated fine hand movements, bench work, and close visual inspection can be demanding. Microtomes have sharp blades, and tissue preparation uses chemicals requiring laboratory safety practices. Protective equipment, safe handling, and the laboratory's chemical-control procedures are part of the job.

The emotional responsibility comes partly from working with material that may be limited or difficult to replace. A careful worker must be willing to report a problem rather than hide it to keep the workflow moving. [O*NET work context](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/29-2011.04); [NSH laboratory safety resources](https://www.nsh.org/Resources/)

## Education and Training

A bachelor's-level science foundation is central to ASCP's HTL pathway. For its current Route 1, candidates need a bachelor's degree with a combined 30 semester hours in biology and chemistry, including both subjects, plus completion of a NAACLS-accredited histotechnician or histotechnologist program within the preceding five years.

This is one certification route, not the only possible education history. Students should compare how a program integrates science courses and supervised histology practice. A program preparing a histotechnician is not automatically the entire bachelor's-level HTL pathway. [ASCP HTL eligibility](https://www.ascp.org/boc/explore-credentials/view-all-credentials/HTL)

## Certification and Licensure

ASCP now names its HTL credential **Scientist in Histotechnology**. Career and employer materials may still use “histotechnologist.” The HT histotechnician credential is distinct from HTL; applicants must meet the requirements for the credential they seek.

Professional certification is also different from laboratory regulation. CLIA establishes federal standards for clinical laboratory testing, while employer duties and applicable state requirements must be checked for a specific role. In Kansas, KDHE administers the CLIA program through its Laboratory Improvement Program Office. A laboratory's CLIA certification is not the same thing as an individual's HTL credential. [ASCP](https://www.ascp.org/boc/explore-credentials/view-all-credentials/HTL); [CMS CLIA](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/quality/clinical-laboratory-improvement-amendments); [KDHE](https://www.kdhe.ks.gov/1114/Clinical-Laboratory-Improvement-Amendmen)

## Salary, Entry-Level Earnings, and Job Outlook

BLS reports a **May 2025 U.S. median annual wage of $62,930 for clinical laboratory technologists and technicians combined**. This broad laboratory benchmark is not a histotechnologist-only figure, not a Kansas wage, and not an entry-level offer. It combines workers whose education and testing responsibilities differ.

The same broad group has projected **3% employment growth from 2025 to 2035**. For a histology position, compare local postings that specify HTL or histotechnologist duties and distinguish routine bench work from lead, specialist, or supervisory responsibilities. [BLS laboratory wage and outlook](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/clinical-laboratory-technologists-and-technicians.htm)

## Specialties and Career Advancement

Modern histology can include immunohistochemistry, which uses antibody-based methods to highlight selected targets in tissue. Digital pathology and molecular methods also connect tissue preparation with additional forms of analysis. These areas add learning opportunities because the preparation must fit the method's needs.

Experienced histotechnologists may teach, supervise laboratory activities, or help develop and troubleshoot techniques. Moving into a new specialty means learning its quality controls and limitations, not simply acquiring a new job title. [NSH modern histotechnology](https://www.nsh.org/about/about-histotechnology); [O*NET responsibilities](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/29-2011.04)

## Benefits and Challenges of the Career

The work offers a close connection between scientific technique and useful evidence. Someone who enjoys making a difficult process work consistently may appreciate seeing a well-prepared tissue section reveal structures clearly.

The challenge is that small preparation errors can have larger consequences. A rushed section or an unchecked staining problem can delay the next step in diagnosis. Satisfaction often comes from quiet, repeatable accuracy rather than public recognition or frequent patient conversations. [O*NET](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/29-2011.04); [Mayo Clinic](https://college.mayo.edu/academics/explore-health-care-careers/careers-a-z/histology-technician/)

## Career Connections and Related Occupations

Histotechnicians and histotechnologists share many tissue-preparation techniques, but their education, credentials, and assigned responsibilities can differ. Histotechnologists may take on additional troubleshooting, method evaluation, or supervision.

Medical laboratory technicians commonly test blood and other body fluids across clinical laboratory disciplines. A pathologist is a physician who interprets tissue and other diagnostic evidence. Histotechnology is especially suited to students interested in the link between tissue structure and disease, with a strong emphasis on preparation quality. [O*NET histotechnologists](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/29-2011.04); [BLS laboratory roles](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/clinical-laboratory-technologists-and-technicians.htm); [NSH](https://www.nsh.org/about/about-histotechnology)

## Could This Career Interest You?

Do you enjoy careful work with small objects and visible details? When a result looks wrong, are you curious about which step caused the problem? Would you find it meaningful to contribute to a diagnosis even if you rarely met the patient?

A laboratory tour or conversation with a histology educator can help you explore these questions. Ask to hear how staff recognize a technically poor slide and what they do next. The answer connects the science to the judgment that makes this profession valuable. [O*NET duties](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/29-2011.04); [NSH education resources](https://www.nsh.org/home)

## Professional Associations

The National Society for Histotechnology is a professional community focused on histology. Its educational resources, publications, and meetings can introduce students to both established techniques and newer developments in tissue science.

The American Society for Clinical Pathology also provides a professional home for laboratory medicine; its Board of Certification administers HT and HTL credentials. Professional participation and credential eligibility are related parts of a career, but membership itself does not certify a person's skills. [NSH](https://www.nsh.org/home); [ASCP Board of Certification](https://www.ascp.org/boc/explore-credentials/view-all-credentials/HTL)
